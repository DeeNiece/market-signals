How the signals work

MA cross: the simple 9-period average closes above the simple 26-period average, and is still above it at the latest close.

HMA turn: the 26-period Hull MA stops falling and starts rising, and is still rising at the latest close. No minimum size is applied, so a turn on a flat line can be weak.

Price vs HMA 26: whether the latest close is above the Hull MA. 9 vs 26 SMA: the gap between the two averages. Prices: raw daily closes, not adjusted for dividends. A stock that went ex-dividend in the last 10 weeks can look weaker here than on a dividend-adjusted chart. Since MA cross / Since HMA turn: price change from the close on the day of the last 9/26 cross, and of the last Hull MA turn up, to the latest close. Shown while the cross or turn is still in force, even if it is older than 12 candles. Distance from 52-wk high: how far the latest close is below the highest price of the last 52 weeks.

Monitoring: a stock is added when it shows a fresh or earlier signal. It stays on the list while the 9 SMA is above the 26 SMA or the Hull MA 26 is rising, even after its signal is older than 12 candles. When the 9 SMA is below the 26 SMA and the Hull MA is falling, it is removed and shown under Removed from monitoring for that scan.

Earnings: shown only when results are due within 7 days of the scan date, because signals around earnings are less reliable.

Fundamentals line: numbers from TradingView. EPS and revenue vs est. compare the last report with analyst estimates. Revenue 12m and EPS 12m are growth over the last twelve months. n/m means not meaningful (a tiny base gives huge percentages). Data can lag a day or two after a company reports, and is shown for reference only.

Averages are calculated from TradingView daily closes. TradingView's own indicators may differ slightly. This page is a signal list for reference, not investment advice.
